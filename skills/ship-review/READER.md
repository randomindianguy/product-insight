# Designing for the reader

Reference. Reached from `/ship-review` when the artifact has a page, a demo, or any surface a hiring manager moves through.

## Segment the reader

A person at a point in time plus a context, never a person. The same hiring manager at 7am triaging fifty applications and at 2pm reading a forwarded pitch is two customers with two specs.

- **Cold scan.** Resume link, seven to ten seconds, tired, no trust. Only the functional job is awake. Everything below the fold does not exist.
- **Warm land.** Arrived from a pitch or a referral, with intent, will scroll.

Name the context being optimised for, and name the one being given up. Both means neither.

## Three jobs, firing at once on the warm land

- **Functional.** Pay off the pitch fast. Prove within seconds that it works.
- **Social.** Arm the champion. A hiring manager cannot hire anyone alone. They have to say something about the user to a room. Hand them the forwardable sentence.
- **Emotional.** Kill the suspicion. They have been burned by polished demos that fall apart.

An artifact that serves only the functional job is a demo nobody fights for.

## Setup, core action, aha

The **aha** is the first moment the reader gets something they could not have got otherwise.

**B = MAT, run on the reader.** Motivation runs high: they want to hire someone good and fear missing them. Ability is the constraint: seven seconds, low mental effort. Trigger is the pitch. So minimise ability cost above the fold, and spend the reader's effort only after motivation has been raised.

**Friction at the aha is the mechanism.** Reduce-friction advice is written for a consumer who owes you nothing and will bounce. This reader arrived with intent and is deciding whether to trust you. Effort they invest becomes belief they own.

## The bet

Requires a **discrimination boundary**: a call with a right answer that a competent person can plausibly get wrong. Detection, triage, classification, review. Where the artifact has no such boundary, the bet becomes a gimmick and costs more than it earns. Skip it.

Where it applies:

- **One bet, never a quiz.** A single sharp call a confident person leans into. The instant a second question appears you have built homework.
- **A bet is fair when the loser had the information to win.** Withhold the facts and the loss feels rigged. Hand over the facts pre-resolved and the answer is arithmetic. Judgment lives in the middle: the decisive fact is visible and unresolved.
- **The reveal credits the builder.** The sentence that lands has the user as the actor and a decision as the object. *I built it to catch it*, never *the agent caught it*. Otherwise the reader loses to a clever machine, and nobody hires a machine.

## Liveness

Real inference at read time reopens cost, invites a hallucination in front of the hiring manager, and contradicts the reliability thesis. Replayed and labelled is the professional choice.

## Voice

Deferential about their product. Commanding about your own work.

Explaining signals command. Convincing signals anxiety. So the single-pass eval gets stated, not apologised for. The finding gets stated, not hedged.

The reader outranks the user on the subject of their own company. Strut at their expense and they stop rooting for you. The car salesman lets you feel fast in the driver's seat. He does not tell you that you drive like your grandmother.

Warm open, generous middle, hardest clause last.

> Don't feel bad, every tool in this category would have called this one fine too.
> I built mine not to.
